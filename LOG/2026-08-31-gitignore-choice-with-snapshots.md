# [HASH] — plan — the gitignore arc: method files leave git on the user's decision, with the plugin's own snapshots replacing git's undo

The user's decision opened it, in her words: having method files be public is not worth the headache — "enough is enough" — all method files gitignored from the next build onwards. Claude cited the record before agreeing: the per-document gitignore offer already exists at setup, and git tracking is what makes write-first safe. Two alternatives were offered and lost on her grounds. Private repositories as the route died on the product's core case — her point, and she was right: consumers use Throughliner to build public-facing work, so a private-repo default makes no sense (the offering of that option after her decision was itself captured as a verbosity failure, [verbosity-pointless-options], since merged). Encryption in the repo (git-crypt/SOPS) lost on key management being unworkable for non-coders, filenames and commit messages still leaking, and history reads likely returning ciphertext — asserted at evidence level, unverified, not worth verifying while the simpler route stood. The design then refined in discussion to a choice at setup rather than a mandatory flip — gitignored as the default on her later ruling the same day — with the plugin supplying recoverability itself: a copy of the previous version saved to a local gitignored snapshot folder before any change to an untracked method document, so write-first and undo survive. The scrub rule moves to the boundary on her instruction ("there needs to be a rule that stops the scrubbing when things are gitignored"): scrubbing fires where content enters a tracked document or leaves the machine, and not before — an amendment to the scrub rule's own purpose, its recorded instance the tester-data path-scrubbing debate. SPEC's privacy-posture sentences were rewritten twice this session, ahead of the build: snapshots as the recoverability, then private-by-default.
