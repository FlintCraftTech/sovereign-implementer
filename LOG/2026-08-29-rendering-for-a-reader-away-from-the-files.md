# 7b751b6 — plan — one spoken trigger for a reader who cannot open the file, and the user caught a draft that would have made Claude interrogate her

Raised mid-walkthrough on remote control: *"sorry can you please give it to me as a card?"* The render rule points at files unconditionally, which assumes the reader could open one — fine for a single queue item, not for a fourteen-part deliverable being approved piece by piece.

**Half of it was already settled by precedent.** The show-first rules considered remote control and refused to make it a detection trigger, on the ground that nothing should be built to reach an outcome that asking reaches. What was missing was not detection but the option: nothing told a session that rendering it another way was available, so it was reached for by improvisation.

**The user caught a defect in Claude's own wording, and it is the reason to record this.** A draft of the rule read "render it where the reader is, by whatever route the session has". Her objection: that forces Claude to infer or ask where she is, defeating the purpose and re-creating the over-asking this method has spent months removing. Corrected to one spoken trigger — she says she cannot open the file, and doc-resident text comes into the message for the rest of that chat — inheriting the show-first block's shape exactly: nothing stored, switch moves one way.

**Refused: naming the mechanism.** Publishing the list as a page is what actually worked, and it stays out of the rule, because writing it in would be a capability claim about every consumer's setup — the reverse-direction check admitted the day before.

**Rule gate: run — an amendment with an eviction.** The absolute "no user override" sentence is the thing being contradicted, so it comes out in the same edit rather than standing beside its own exception.

**It is a class, not one instance** — the same gap appears for any long approval set put to a reader away from the machine.
