**4. Communicating your port's flavour**

Say which of two things your port is, wherever you present it. It costs one
line and it is the difference between a port people can choose and a port
nobody can read.

```
TRACKING       accepts this project's changes at face value, and adds no new
               ones of its own beyond what its own system required to fit.

INDEPENDENT    unique and its own thing, which may or may not accept changes
               derived from the changelogs.
```

**Both are welcome and neither is the lesser one.** You should have the option
of running a Throughliner led by this project and carrying all its features so
far as your setup allows — and diverging, renaming and developing it yourself
is equally fine.

**Why it matters in three directions.** Someone choosing between ports cannot
otherwise tell whether the one they install follows the method or has gone its
own way. A porter has no way to signal which they are doing. And this project
cannot tell which ports its changelog is even for.

**Where to say it:** your README, first screen. A line is enough — "a tracking
port of Throughliner, currently pinned to v1.21.1" tells a reader everything
they need, including how far behind you are.

**No register of who runs which flavour is kept here, deliberately.**
Maintaining a list of other people's projects is a standing obligation this
project cannot keep accurate, and being absent from such a list would read as
disapproval. The flavour is something a port declares about itself.

**Changing your mind is allowed and needs no permission.** A port that starts
tracking and later diverges just says so — the declaration describes what you
are doing now, not a promise about the future.

The definitions live in `docs/ports.md` in the repository, which is the
canonical wording if you want to quote it.
