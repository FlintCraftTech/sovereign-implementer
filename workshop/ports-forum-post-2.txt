**2. Starting your own port — from inside Throughliner**

The route post 1 points at: adopt the method for the port itself. Your port
becomes a Throughliner project, so every mapping decision arrives as a queue
item with its reasoning attached, and why you did it that way outlives the
session that decided it.

**How to turn it on.** At a fresh `/setup`, the interview asks whether you are
building something that will carry its own rules — a method, a plugin, a port.
Say yes. On a project already set up, say "I want to self-host" at any time and
the same seed runs against it as it stands. It is add-only: nothing you wrote
is rewritten, and where a file it would create already exists, it says so and
leaves it alone.

**What it seeds** — a block in your project's CLAUDE.md, between its own
markers, carrying:

- **The rule gate** — admission, eviction, distribution, wording. The part that
  matters most on a port: every rule you add degrades the rules already there,
  because near-identical rules are optimal distractors for one another. So a
  new rule names the parent it amends, and names what comes out.
- **The disposition pattern** — each rule decision recorded on the queue item
  that carries it, with a line in the session record. A gate that runs after
  the work has no power to refuse.
- **Host versus target** — the code you are editing versus the copy actually
  running your session. Ports live and die on this one.

Plus two files: a retired-terms register and an audit checklist.

**What it deliberately does not seed:** this project's release and packaging
rituals, and its rule-checking scripts. They are shaped around one repository's
layout. The discipline generalises; the machinery does not — build your own
once your gate has a few decisions on record.

**A prompt for the first session after seeding:**

> Read the self-hosting block in CLAUDE.md, then our port's own rules, and
> tell me which of them would fail the gate's admission test today.
