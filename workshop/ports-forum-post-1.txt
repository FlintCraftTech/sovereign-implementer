**1. Starting your own port — from outside Throughliner**

Point an agent at the repo, say "make a port", and by the afternoon you will
have something that looks like Throughliner. Do it — it beats a blank folder.
Just know what you are holding: the start of a long trail, not the summit, and
not even base camp.

What decides whether it ever works is what comes after. Every rule here
was argued out and recorded, and porting means going through them one at a
time. @coughfee spent two solid weeks on a Codex port and got nowhere — digging
and digging and arriving on Saturn. Sparing porters that is this forum's point.

**The honest cost.** Around two hours a day for the first month, easing after.
@coughfee spends three to four a day, seven days a week, on the original.

**What you're porting.** Three things; only the third is hard.

- **Five procedures** — setup, plan, next, rescan, done. Plain markdown in
  `docs/`. Instructions to a model, so they carry as text.
- **Three project documents** — SPEC (what it is), QUEUE (what's next, and
  why), LOG (what happened). Just files.
- **Four scripts firing at moments in a session** — `session_start`,
  `pre_tool_use`, `post_tool_use`, `stop`. Your tool has its own moments;
  mapping ours onto them is judgement, and some may have no equivalent.

**Start by reading.** `docs/skill-nonspecific-rules.md` is fed into every
session and describes the method at bottom. Try:

> Read docs/skill-nonspecific-rules.md and docs/plan.md. List what each
> assumes about the tool it runs inside — what fires automatically, what the
> user types, what touches a file. Don't map anything yet.

**The recommended route: run the method on your port.** Say yes to `/setup`'s
self-hosting question — or "I want to self-host" on a project already set up —
and each decision arrives as an item to weigh, not a diff to accept. Post 2
covers what that seeds.

**Say which flavour yours is** — tracking or independent. `docs/ports.md`.
